Your AI coding seats, as a living office.

Claude Office turns every Claude Code account and the Codex CLI on your machine into a pixel-art office. See who is working and who is stuck in one glance, talk to any task, and split new work across your seats.

What you see

One office. Every seat. Every session.

Every Claude Code account on your machine gets a desk, and so does the Codex CLI. Every running session is a little worker at that desk, acting out what it is doing right now. You run the place from the CEO's chair.

The floor

A calm floor you can read in one glance

The header sums up the office in plain English: who is working, who is out of plan and until when, and where the next lane would go.

  • Each desk's nameplate says who it is, what it's doing and how much plan is left, in words: "3 working · 36% of 5-hour left".
  • Workers type, read, think and wait on long tools, sweat when stuck, and fall asleep when their seat is out of plan.
  • A "Right now" strip of cards under the floor shows who is at work and what each one is doing.
  • Pixel the office cat wanders the corridors and naps in the lounge. Click her for a tip.
Talk to a task

Open any session and talk to it

The Chat tab shows the conversation: prompts, replies, collapsed tool steps, messages from other sessions, and replies sent to you.

  • Send a message from the office. Lanes you hire here get it straight away; terminal sessions hold it until you approve it at that terminal.
  • Agents can answer you. Replies show up in the chat and as a toast.
  • Follow up on a finished session: your message starts a lane that resumes that exact conversation.
  • Message all lanes at once, for example "limits reset in 20 minutes: write your notes now".
Characters

Every seat is a character

A name, a role, a motto and a few traits. Atlas, The Investigator: "Show me the evidence." Rename them and change their colours in Settings.

  • A mood that follows the live state: In the zone, Pacing myself, Running on fumes, Napping until 1:40 AM, On holiday, Locked out.
  • They speak up when it matters: when they arrive, when they ship, when they hit a limit, and when a fresh window opens.
  • Click a desk's nameplate to meet its character and see its plan.
Team

The whole team on one page

A card for every character, with both plan windows and everyone at that desk.

  • Who is in the zone, who is pacing themselves, and who is napping or on holiday.
  • How much of the 5-hour window and the week is left, and when each one resets.
  • Every session at each desk, and what it is doing.
Timeline

The last 24 hours, and what is coming

Every session on every seat over the last 1 to 24 hours, coloured by state, with usage, news markers, upcoming resets, and a ✕ where a seat runs out at its current pace.

Today

An employee of the day, and a league

A league table of time at work, sessions, reports, limits, tool calls, tokens and peak usage, and a short digest of the day.

Load divider

Share new work across seats

Drag the borders of the split bar to decide which seat gets the next lane.

  • Send a seat on holiday, cap its lanes, or set usage guards.
  • Adapt-to-plan mode shrinks a seat's share as its plan runs out; a fixed split keeps yours.
  • See who gets the next lane, and why, seat by seat.
  • Scripts follow it too: seat=$(claude-office pick-seat)
Hiring desk

Hire a lane from the front desk

Start a background lane from one of six brief templates, or re-hire a past one.

  • Lanes run as low-priority background services.
  • A lane keeps notes, moves to another seat if its seat hits a limit, and files a report when it is done.
  • Stop, resume, re-hire or chat with any lane.
How it works

Up and running in a minute

No package manager, no database, no account to create. Clone it, start it, and keep working the way you already do.

  1. 01 · INSTALL

    Clone the repository

    You need Python 3.10 or newer and nothing else: the server uses only the standard library, and the page has no build step.

    $ git clone https://github.com/JigarJoshi04/claude-office
$ cd claude-office
  2. 02 · OPEN

    Start the office

    Open the page it prints. Want to look around first? Demo mode reads no accounts, and every write does nothing.

    $ python3 -m office serve
# http://127.0.0.1:7420
$ python3 -m office serve --demo
  3. 03 · RUN YOUR SEATS

    Work as usual

    Seats in ~/.claude, ~/.claude2, … and ~/.codex are found on their own, and every live session walks in. Keep the office running as a service:

    $ bin/install.sh
# a systemd user service

Linux is needed for the live view: sessions are read from /proc and lanes run under systemd --user.

Features

Everything a CEO of AI seats needs

Plain tools for a busy floor: what is happening, what needs you, and where the next piece of work should go.

A desk for every seat

~/.claude, ~/.claude2, … and ~/.codex are found automatically. Each desk's nameplate says what that seat is doing and how much plan it has left.

A worker for every session

Typing, reading, thinking or waiting on a long tool. Terminal sessions wear headsets; a subagent shows up as a little helper drone.

An office that feels alive

Arrivals walk in from the elevator, departures walk out, filed reports burst into confetti and spent seats get a red beacon. Night lighting follows your clock, and Pixel the office cat naps in the lounge.

The CEO's briefing

Who is working, who is lowest on plan and when it resets, and where the next lane goes, in plain sentences. Then your team, programmes, office lanes and recent activity.

A "Needs you" list

Logouts, seats near empty, lanes stopped by a limit, quiet lanes, blocked reports, failed services, and seats that will run dry before their reset at the current pace.

A camera

Zoom and pan the floor, and double-click a desk to fly to it. Zoomed in, every worker wears a name tag.

Command palette and checkpoints

Ctrl/⌘ K jumps to any seat, session or lane. A checkpoint writes every session and its resume command to a file, for before you restart.

Share-safe mode

Blurs what terminal sessions are about, and account emails, so you can put the office on a screen share.

Small delights

8-bit sound effects, desktop notifications, optional Jarvis voice alerts, a day theme, and reduced motion.

Safety

Your machine. Your tokens. Nobody else's.

Claude Office reads sensitive things, so it is built to keep them where they are. Transcripts are read locally and stay on your machine.

Loopback only

The server listens on 127.0.0.1 and rejects any request whose Host is not loopback, which stops DNS-rebinding pages.

A token on every write

Every write must carry a per-install token, which only the office's own page receives.

Strict Content-Security-Policy

Transcript text is escaped, and credential-shaped strings are masked before they reach the page.

Credentials never leave the server

They are read only at the moment of a usage call, and never sent to the page, logged or cached. The office never refreshes a login, so it never rotates a token under Claude Code.

Messages only when you send them

The office's inbox accepts connections only from your own user. It never claims a permission mode, and it never sends a message without your action.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Does it send my code or transcripts anywhere?

No. Transcripts are read on your machine, only the new bytes since the last look, and the server listens on 127.0.0.1 only. The one request the server sends off your machine goes to each seat's plan usage endpoint, every few minutes, with that seat's own access token, to show how much plan is left.

The page loads its fonts from Google Fonts; nothing else leaves the browser.

Which tools and accounts does it support?

Claude Code accounts in ~/.claude, ~/.claude2, ~/.claude3 and so on, plus the Codex CLI in ~/.codex. Each folder that holds a login becomes a seat with its own desk pod.

What do I need to run it?

Python 3.10 or newer, and nothing else: no pip packages, no Node, no build step. Linux is needed for the live view, because sessions are read from /proc and lanes run under systemd --user.

Can the office get around Claude Code's review of messages?

No. Claude Code sessions listen on a private Unix socket for messages from other sessions, and the office writes the same one-line frame to it, labelled as coming from "Claude Office (CEO)". The receiving session decides what happens:

A lane hired from the office with "Let me message it" on (the default) is started to accept such messages, so it gets yours straight away. A terminal session holds it until someone approves it at that terminal. A finished session gets a follow-up lane that resumes it with claude --resume. The office never claims a permission mode, so it cannot skip that review.

Will it log my seats out or rotate my tokens?

No. The office never refreshes a login itself, because a refresh would rotate the token under Claude Code. While a seat's token is expired, it shows the last reading, marked stale.

Can I try it without my real accounts?

Yes. Run python3 -m office serve --demo for a made-up office: it reads no accounts, and every write does nothing. The video and screenshots on this page are all demo mode.
